Drought Drives Down EU Rapeseed Acreage and Harvest Estimates

Due to a severe drought that complicated late-summer planting, EU rapeseed acreage for the 2027/28 MY is projected to drop by about 2%, falling from 6.4 million hectares in 2026/27 to roughly 6.3 million hectares. France faces the sharpest reduction, with areas potentially dropping by 10–15% due to record soil drought and delayed rainfall. Despite high prices that initially prompted farmers to expand planting, moisture deficits have altered these plans, creating next-season supply risks. The 2026/27 EU rapeseed harvest estimate was reduced to 19.8 million tons from 20 million tons due to weaker yields in Germany and Poland, and the soybean forecast was cut to 2.3 million tons. Conversely, the EU sunflower production forecast rose to 9.7 million tons, driven by higher estimates for Bulgaria, Romania, and Slovakia.

Rapeseed Market

European Rapeseed Prices Stabilize Amid Supply Tightness

Rapeseed prices in Europe stabilized after a brief rally, supported by long-term tightness in the Black Sea region and steady regional demand. At the end of the week, November rapeseed futures on Euronext in Paris settled at €548.00/t, down 0.27% daily but maintaining a 1.81% gain over the past month. One of the key supportive factors remains the structural deficit of high-quality rapeseed supply from Ukraine and Russia, which keeps prompt processing margins for European crushers stable. As of late September, physical spot prices for European rapeseed deliverable to processing hubs tightly tracked the €548–550/t corridor.

Soybean Futures Drop on CBOT Due to Macro Pressures

Macro Pressure on Soybeans

Soybeans fell to $12.97 per bushel ($1,297.07 USd/Bu) on September 28, 2026, dropping 1.66% as macro pressures weighed on the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT). Despite the recent multi-day slide, prices remain up 1.71% over the past month and significantly higher than last year's levels, balanced by ongoing Chinese import demand and shifting global supply cushions.

Ukraine Lost Status as World's No. 1 Sunflower Oil Exporter
On September 14, it was reported that Ukraine has lost its status as the world’s leading sunflower oil exporter for the first time in 20 seasons (since 2005/06). According to the September USDA Foreign Agricultural Service report, Russia became the largest global exporter in the 2025/26 marketing year. Meanwhile, Ukraine's export forecast for the new 2026/27 season was slightly increased to 5 million tons.
Ukraine

Food Price Inflation & Domestic Market

In August 2026, Ukrainian food prices remained higher year-on-year. Sunflower oil saw the sharpest increase at 21.8%, while overall food and non-alcoholic beverage prices rose by 6%. Animal fats grew by 12.9%, milk and cheese up 6.9% and 4.9% respectively. Meat prices rose slightly by 1.3%, while butter became 1.6% cheaper. Conversely, egg prices dropped by 24.1% and sugar by 5.1%. With annual inflation accelerating to 8.1%, food price dynamics remained mixed, driven primarily by the significant surge in sunflower oil costs against a backdrop of declining prices for eggs and sugar.

Sunflower Prices Decline & New Crop

The country's harvest campaign is underway, with rapeseed harvesting now 100% complete. Due to the active influx of new crop supplies into the market, sunflower purchase prices decreased by 500–1,000 UAH/t over the past week (to 18,700–19,500 UAH/t including VAT, delivered to plants). As a result, for the first time in two years, sunflower seeds have become cheaper than rapeseed.

Rail Logistics Shift

Due to blockades and attacks, the Greater Odesa ports have been closed to stable oilseed exports since late July. Consequently, logistics have shifted to rail and river routes. According to Ukrzaliznytsia, August rail exports of vegetable oil surged 54.7% year-on-year and 15.4% from July, reaching 113,000 tons. A clear imbalance has emerged: 85% of rail oil shipments are directed toward western border crossings to the EU, with only 15% heading to accessible ports (primarily the Danube). A similar trend (79% vs 21%) is observed for meal and cake exports.

September Export Performance

During the first ten days of September 2026, Ukraine exported 959,000 tons of agricultural products worth approximately $493 million, with grains accounting for 54.2% of the volume. Turkey, Germany, Italy, Algeria, and Poland remained the primary destinations, receiving 43% of the total tonnage. Road transport moved 113,700 tons, predominantly toward Poland and Romania, while rail shipments of grain reached 376,700 tons, showing a 15.2% increase over August levels despite a year-on-year decline. Rail logistics are increasingly focused on western border crossings, particularly toward Hungary and Slovakia, while port-bound rail traffic is heavily concentrated on the Danube region due to minimal activity in the "Big Odesa" ports.

Russia

Record Sunflower Yields and Harvest

Russian farmers are experiencing record sunflower yields in 2026, averaging 19 centners per hectare compared to 12.8 c/ha last year, driven by favorable weather conditions and sufficient soil moisture. Domestic hybrids show even higher efficiency, reaching up to 31–35 c/ha in regions like Voronezh, Krasnodar, and Kherson. With the harvest underway on less than 10% of the area and pest damage remaining a primary risk, total sowing areas reached roughly 13.3 million hectares, led by the Saratov region. USDA and RUSEED analysts project the 2026/27 harvest at 21 million tons, up from 17 million tons in 2025.

Surge in Russian Agricultural Exports to Malaysia

Russia significantly expanded its agricultural exports to Malaysia, increasing physical volume 5-fold and monetary value 5.5-fold between January and August 2026 compared to the full year of 2025, reaching 8.8 thousand tons worth nearly $9 million. The primary drivers were rapeseed and soybean oils. Meanwhile, domestic consumption of vegetable oil in Russia remained robust, with the retail market growing by 10% in the first half of the year, while domestic producers covered approximately 99% of internal market demand.

Sharp Drop in Sunflower Oil Production

In August 2026, the production of unrefined sunflower oil in Russia dropped sharply by 32.4% year-on-year to 0.3 million tons, driven by a 34.1% monthly decrease. This contraction was primarily caused by the virtual halt of vegetable oil exports through the Azov-Black Sea basin following the late July terminal attack in Taman, which forced processors to accumulate stock and cut capacity utilization, despite overall production for the first eight months remaining up by 2.2% to 4.7 million tons.

Romania and Bulgaria

Expansion of Sunflower Processing Capacity in Bulgaria

Bulgaria continues to ramp up its domestic sunflower processing capacity and vegetable oil manufacturing. Strong local crushing trends reflect active regional processing demand within the European and Black Sea agricultural markets, allowing processors to optimize raw material usage despite shifting trade patterns and supply adjustments across neighboring origin markets.

Sharp Decline in Slovakia's Corn and Sunflower Stockpiles

Data for the first half of the year highlights a sharp contraction in corn and sunflower stockpiles in Slovakia. This notable drop in domestic grain and oilseed inventories underscores tighter domestic supplies and shifting regional availability, impacting local processing operations and commercial balances within the Central European market.

Kazakhstan

Vegetable Oil Production and Export Growth

Kazakhstan has recorded significant growth in the production and export of vegetable oil. From January to July, output increased by 29.1% (reaching 673.2 thousand tons), while exports grew by 46.2% in the first half of the year (reaching 560.6 thousand tons). Sunflower oil accounts for the bulk of this volume, with the country ranking sixth globally in sunflower oil exports and second among suppliers to China based on 2025 results. Oilseed crop cultivation areas expanded to 5.2 million hectares. Despite rising production volumes, domestic prices for vegetable oil continue to increase.

Launch of Agro-Industrial Complex "QazEcoDan"

A modern agro-industrial complex named "QazEcoDan" for receiving, cleaning, drying, and storing grain and oilseed crops has been commissioned in the Enbekshikazakh district of the Almaty region, with a total project cost of 21 billion tenge. The enterprise is equipped with German processing technology and Spanish storage silos, providing a storage capacity of up to 30,000 tons.

Railway Tariff Increase

The Committee for Regulation of Natural Monopolies has approved a tariff increase for mainline railway network (MRN) services following an application by Kazakhstan Temir Zholy (KTZ). Starting September 21, 2026, the rate increased by 24%, with no indexation planned for 2027, and an annual indexation of 3% scheduled for 2028–2030.

New Functional Spread "W-balance"

Kazakh scientists from the Research Institute of Processing and Food Industry have created a new functional spread called "W-balance" with a 1:5 ratio Omega-3 to Omega-6 fatty acids. The formulation utilizes domestically produced milk fat, rapeseed oil, and flaxseed oil. Technical documentation has been fully prepared, and the development complies Customs Union technical regulations, allowing it to be adapted on existing production lines for dietary and therapeutic-prophylactic nutrition.

Uzbekistan

Legislative and Socio-Economic Changes from October 1

A number of new rules will come into effect in Uzbekistan starting October 1, 2026. To support businesses, export customs duties will be reduced by 30%, importers will be allowed to offset VAT, and small businesses will gain access to preferential lending.

Uzbek-Syrian Business Forum

On September 19, an Uzbek-Syrian Business Forum was held in Tashkent, attended by Uzbekistan's Minister of Investment, Industry and Trade Laziz Kudratov and Syria's Minister of Economy and Industry Mohammad Nidal al-Shaar. The Syrian side was represented by heads of more than 30 major companies operating in textiles, pharmaceuticals, food and chemical industries, agriculture, construction materials, electrical engineering, and other sectors. A signed memorandum establishing a Joint Economic and Trade Committee, along with a newly formed Business Council, laid the institutional groundwork for regular engagement between government bodies and businesses of the two countries. In February 2027, a "Made in Uzbekistan" exhibition is planned for Aleppo and Damascus, with an exhibition of Syrian products in Tashkent later that year. The business program continued with B2B and B2G meetings, and the two sides set up a working group to prepare a unified roadmap for cooperation.

General Structure of Uzbekistan's External Imports

Overall, from January to July 2026, the total volume of Uzbekistan's imports grew by 17.8% compared to the previous year, reaching $29.6 billion. The leading position (about a third of the total volume) is occupied by machinery and transport equipment, imports of which amounted to $9.7 billion (+20%). A significant share of imports is also formed by industrial goods ($4.4 billion), chemicals and services ($3.6 billion each), and food products ($3.3 billion). The import of animal and vegetable oils, fats, and waxes amounted to $363 million, showing an increase of 25.3%.

Azerbaijan

Importer Registries & Surge in Shipments from Russia

The Food Safety Agency of the Republic of Azerbaijan (AQTA) issued updated lists of foreign facilities approved for food imports. The registry now includes new foreign crushing plants and meal producers. Inclusion in this database remains a strict requirement for raw and refined oils to cross the border. The Ministry of Agriculture of the Nizhny Novgorod Region (a key Russian supplying region) reported on its export structure. Oil and fat shipments to Azerbaijan during the first eight months of the year surged by 70.3% in volume.

Dairy Fat Shortage

Due to a 10% drop in domestic butter production, the State Customs Committee recorded urgent import substitution. Imports of vegetable fats and oils (primarily sunflower and palm oils) grew by 17.8% (up by 15.7 thousand tons) to meet the demands of the confectionery sector and HoReCa.

Logistics and Supply Security

Caspian Port Cargo Turnover Growth

Caspian ports cargo turnover reached 6.2 million tons in 2024. It is expected to grow to 8 million tons in 2026 and 35 million tons by 2030. The event will showcase the "Lotos" special economic zone, new grain and container terminals, and infrastructure projects valued at $38 billion. The primary export demand includes Russian grain, coal, chemicals, and paper for Asian and African markets.

Sea Terminal Crisis and Export Redirection

According to analytical reports the average cost of sunflower oil in the domestic Black Sea market dropped by 9.8% in a single week. Experts directly attribute this to physical restrictions on exports: the operation of traditional bulk terminals in the Azov and Black Sea ports was effectively paralyzed due to military risks and infrastructure attacks. Industry publications recorded a massive restructuring of supply chains. Due to bottlenecks and hazards in the Black Sea, exporters began urgently shifting bulk shipments of vegetable oils and meals to terminals in St. Petersburg, Kaliningrad, Ust-Luga, and Murmansk.

Launch of Uzbekistan's Mega-Project in Georgia

On September 21, 2026, the administration of the President of Uzbekistan, Shavkat Mirziyoyev, officially announced the signing of a strategic agreement between Uzbekistan and Georgia. The agreement provides for the creation of Uzbekistan's own logistical hubs, terminals, and warehouses directly on the territory of the Georgian Black Sea port of Poti.

Caspian Connectivity & Alternative Corridors

Within the framework of the Caspian Business Forum held in New York on September 22, 2026, and current reports from the Ministries of Transport of Central Asian countries, the full restoration of regular supply chains for bulk vegetable oils (sunflower and rapeseed) from Kazakhstan via the port of Aktau was confirmed. The route serves as a vital artery for the Middle Corridor. An international consortium of major operators and stakeholders in the Caspian region initiated a large-scale feasibility study to expand an alternative southern corridor. The project entails rerouting cargo flows along the Atyrau – Aktau – Baku – Tbilisi – Ceyhan (Turkey) chain, accompanied by a sharp increase in the Caspian tanker fleet. Although the primary focus remains on hydrocarbons, the infrastructure being created and the new high-tonnage bulk vessels are viewed by the oil and fat sector as a promising foundation for transporting soft oilseeds and oils to Middle Eastern and Southern European markets.

Regulatory Outlook

FEFAC: EU Soybean Meal Gap ≥5 Mt

On September 17, 2026, the European Feed Manufacturers' Federation (FEFAC) published its fourth update on the feed supply chain disruption risk and economic impact of the EUDR on soy supplies for animal feed. The assessment warns that severe drought, Black Sea trade disruption, and EUDR sourcing constraints are converging to squeeze the EU feed chain simultaneously, projecting a potential EU soybean meal supply gap of at least 5 million tonnes — roughly 20% of the EU's total soybean meal footprint — for the 2026/2027 marketing year, with very limited substitution options available. FEFAC estimates the direct extra cost of EUDR-compliant soy for feed use at up to €1.05 billion (about $1.20 billion), based on total usage of around 35 million tonnes; because rapeseed and sunflower meal prices are pegged to soybean meal quotations, the increase adds a further €449 million in costs for these alternative proteins. FEFAC President Nicolas Coudry-Mesny said the imminent application of the EUDR after December 30, 2026 is already disrupting EU soy purchases from global markets, exacerbating pressure from drought and closed maritime corridors. The federation is urging EU institutions to ensure immediate simplified implementation of the EUDR for in-scope soy products and to suspend the EUDR's application to Palm Kernel Expeller (PKE) to safeguard this alternative livestock feed source heading into 2027.

German Bundesrat Adopts Formal Position on EUDR Implementation Framework

On September 25, 2026, the German Bundesrat (Federal Council) adopted its formal position (Drucksache 438/26) regarding the national implementation and enforcement bill for the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR). A key element of the discussion and adopted opinion centers on establishing a firmer legal and administrative basis for simplified joint filings by small forest-owner groups and forestry associations to reduce bureaucratic burdens.

MOAH: Adoption Slipped to October 2026

Contrary to the earlier expectation of a September 2026 adoption, the European Commission's own Food Safety catalogue and multiple industry trackers now confirm that formal adoption of the amendment to Regulation (EU) 2023/915 setting the first harmonized statutory maximum levels for Mineral Oil Aromatic Hydrocarbons (MOAH) — based on the ALARA principle — is targeted for October 2026, subject to scrutiny by the European Parliament and the Council, rather than September as previously reported. The measure, backed by Member States at the May 13, 2026 Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF) vote, is still set to apply generally from January 1, 2027. For oils and fats, limits will be phased down progressively toward 2.0 mg/kg by 2030, while low-fat dry products face a 0.5 mg/kg threshold — figures consistent with the previous outlook, though the adoption date itself has slipped by roughly a month.

Palm Oil Sector Adapts Early to Looming EU MOAH Safety Limits

In late September 2026, industry reports highlighted that major players in the global palm oil supply chain are accelerating safety and refining adjustments to meet upcoming strict European Union limits on Mineral Oil Aromatic Hydrocarbons (MOAH) in food products. Early investments in processing technology and contamination mitigation are giving palm oil exporters an operational advantage as the regulatory enforcement timeline approaches.

Logistics and Supply Security

Afghanistan as Transit Hub

Afghanistan is emerging as a vital transit hub linking Central Asia, Iran, and Turkey. Key projects like the $780 million Herat-Mazar-i-Sharif railway and the "Five Nations Corridor" aim to connect China to the West. These routes offer Afghanistan alternatives to Pakistani ports, while Iran seeks to bypass maritime risks. Uzbekistan is actively coordinating with Kabul and Tehran, integrating its infrastructure to boost regional trade. If realized, this interconnected network will bypass traditional bottlenecks, transforming Afghanistan from a regional barrier into a strategic Eurasian transit link, significantly expanding trade access to the Persian Gulf, Turkey, and the EU.

First Block Train China → Iran

The first block train from China to Iran, organized by Uztemiryulkonteyner, has begun its journey through Uzbekistan. The 55-car train, carrying automotive parts and consumer goods, is traveling over 6,000 km across China, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Turkmenistan. Following its entry via the Khorgos-Altynkol crossing and transit through Uzbekistan, the train is expected to reach the Sarakhs border station in 14–16 days. This new route marks a significant step in establishing regular container block train services along the China–Central Asia–Iran corridor, further integrating regional logistics networks and enhancing trans-Eurasian trade connectivity.

Port of Bar (Montenegro)

Kazakh Ambassador to Montenegro, Daulet Batrashev, visited the Port of Bar to explore using Montenegrin infrastructure for cargo transit between Kazakhstan, the Western Balkans, and Europe. Discussions with port and local officials focused on integrating the Port of Bar into the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route (Middle Corridor). The parties examined potential cooperation in logistics, specifically for transporting Kazakh agricultural products and grain, as well as developing container shipping. Both sides agreed to further evaluate the economic feasibility of joint investment projects and the use of terminal facilities to enhance trade connectivity.

Russia: Zero Duties on Grain

The customs subcommission of the Ministry of Agriculture of the Russian Federation made a fundamental decision to zero duties on wheat, corn, and barley through the end of 2026, a measure that took effect September 1 but continued to draw scrutiny through mid-September, as reported by trade outlets on September 17. Moscow says the move is meant to support exporters after repeated Ukrainian strikes on Black Sea port infrastructure left grain shippers struggling to move the new harvest; before the change, Russia charged 787.5 rubles per tonne on wheat exports and 406.5 rubles per tonne on corn, with barley duties zeroed out as well, while duties on sunflower oil (a major Indian import) remain unchanged at 7,748 rubles per tonne. As of the week of the report, the decision had reportedly still not been formally published in Russia's official gazette — an unusual gap traders were watching closely — reflecting how the Azov-Black Sea basin's shipping constraints continue to disrupt the country's main export corridor.

Ukraine: Settlement Deadline Extension

On a related front, Ukraine's National Bank extended the payment settlement deadline for imports of selected industrial equipment from 180 to 270 days, effective September 17, 2026 — a further easing of foreign-exchange control rules following the National Bank's earlier extension (in late August) of agricultural export settlement periods to 150 days, and the Cabinet of Ministers' related decision allowing the Ministry of Economy to extend settlement deadlines for wheat, corn, soybeans, sunflower seeds, sunflower oil, and oilcake by up to 180 days upon exporter request, both adopted in response to the same Black Sea logistics disruptions.

Caspian Connectivity

On Caspian connectivity, Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan are preparing to resume passenger ferry service on the Kuryk–Baku (Alat) route, Trend.Az reported on September 10, 2026, alongside plans to develop joint AI-based solutions for managing cargo flows, processing documentation, and assessing risk in cross-border trade between Kazakhstan Railways and the Baku International Sea Trade Port at Alat. A 391-kilometer fiber-optic cable across the Caspian Sea, with capacity of up to 400 Tbps, is expected to be completed by the end of 2026, and cargo shipments along the Middle Corridor through Azerbaijan have grown by roughly 90% over the past three years.
